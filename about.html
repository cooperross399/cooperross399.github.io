What the model does

Each model is fit on regular-season results. It estimates expected goals or points for both sides, then works out the odds of each outcome on the board: winner, margin, over or under.

Those odds get converted to a fair price, the line where a bet would break even if the model were right. The board puts fair next to the market's open and current prices. Where they differ is the whole point.

How picks are chosen

Edge is the model's probability minus what the best market price implies, after calibrating against past results. Most edges are small. A lot of small edges are noise.

A best bet has to clear three bars: a minimum edge, a minimum price, and a market I've approved for selection. It gets a tier and a stake in units. A unit is a fixed slice of a notional bankroll. Dollar figures aren't shown.

A lean is the model's side on a game that didn't clear the bars. It's written down so it can be checked later. It isn't staked and doesn't count in the record.

No play means no price reached the build, or nothing cleared. Once a game starts, the pick stays up for the record but it's no longer a play.

How results are graded

The first board published each day is the day's opinion. Results grades that frozen copy against the finals. Nothing gets recomputed afterwards. A bad board stays bad in the Archive.

Straight up: did the projected winner win. Totals: was the projected total on the right side of the market's line. The picks record is best bets only, at the published price.

The season's return on best bets is sealed until a date I set before the season started. A running total on the front page every morning turns a season-long test into a scoreboard, and I don't want to be tempted to touch the model because of a bad week. Until then the boards show how many bets have settled, not how they're doing.

Reading a board

Projected
The model's expected score before the game. Not a prediction of the exact final.
Win %
The model's win probability. The thin bar under each game is the same split in team colours.
Open / Current
The market price when the board was first built, and at the last refresh. The small line under a cell is the movement.
Fair
The model's break-even price. A market price better than fair is where the edge is.
Edge
Model probability minus market-implied probability, after calibration, in percentage points.
Best bet
Cleared every bar. Shown on black with a tier and a stake in units.
Lean
The model's side, recorded but not staked. Shown with a dashed border.
Record
Settled from the frozen boards only. Intervals are 95% confidence. "No demonstrated edge" means zero is inside the interval.
Feed stale
The board hasn't rebuilt in over 30 hours. Don't read it as tonight.
B2B
The team played the previous night.

What it is not

This is a public record of a model. It isn't betting advice and nothing here is a recommendation to bet. The model doesn't place bets and neither do I on its behalf. The units are bookkeeping so the record can be graded.

Nothing on these boards is a proven edge. Telling a real edge from luck takes thousands of settled bets, and the boards show how far along that count is. A good week proves nothing. Neither does a bad one.
