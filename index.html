Six models. Five measured. No demonstrated edge.

One person, six sports betting models, each in its own public repository with its own measurement record. Five have now been measured end to end against real prices. Every one of the five came back with no demonstrated edge, and two college basketball tiers came back with a demonstrated deficit. The sixth, college football, has not been measured at all yet.

That is the finding, and it is the reason this page exists. The models were built to see whether a solo research project could beat the price the market closes at. The answer so far is that it does not, and the record of not doing so is published here in the same detail it would have been published in had it worked.

Nothing here is for sale. There is no subscription, no tip service, no selection feed to join, and no claim anywhere on this site that any of this makes money. What follows is a ledger: every model, every sample size, every verdict, and a link out to the model pages as they go live.

Reading the ledger Three verdicts, and nothing else

No demonstrated edge

The measured return cannot be separated from zero at the sample shown. Where a 95% interval is published it contains zero. This does not say the model loses; it says the model has not been shown to beat the market, which is the only claim the measurement supports.

Demonstrated deficit

Stronger and worse: the measured return sits below zero by more than the sample noise. Two of the three college basketball tiers land here, at −5.5% and −4.6% over a combined 175,690 graded bets.

No verdict

Nothing has been measured yet, so nothing is reported. An empty cell in this ledger means the work has not been done — it is never shorthand for a result that was inconvenient.

Why the null is the headline

The record is the whole product.

A model that does not beat the closing price is the ordinary outcome of this work. It is also the outcome that almost never gets published, because it is worth nothing to sell and everything to bury. Sites like this one usually exist to show you the one line that went right.

So the discipline is the interesting part. Bars are declared before the measurement runs. Samples are reported whole, not trimmed to the stretch that flatters. Intervals are printed next to point estimates, because a point estimate without one is just a number that agrees with you. And a result that comes back null is left exactly as it came back.

What that leaves is six public repositories, a measurement record that says what it actually found, and one live page that publishes no selections at all because no market has earned one. If a model ever does clear its bar, this ledger is where it will have to say so — next to the five entries that did not.
